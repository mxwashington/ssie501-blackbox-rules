[C16] Every Area 1 change is either one step along its branch (0->1->2->3->4 or 9->8->7->6->5) or a jump to the START of the other branch (0-4 -> 9, 5-9 -> 0). Counts (advance / jump / anything else): discovery 27393/7154/0; heldout 13570/3578/0; group2 18/19/0.
[C17] A picked Area 1 cell that is not 4 or 5 always moves; a picked 4 or 5 leaves only 2.5% of the time (held-out; a 4 leaves 1.2%, a 5 leaves 7.5%). 'Every non-4/5 pick moves' plus that exit rate predicts 17,071 changing steps vs 17,148 observed. By the last recorded step 99% of Area 1 cells are 4 or 5 (79% are 4).
[C18] Whether a moving cell jumps is random: 15% of moves by non-4/5 cells are jumps (discovery). It depends on the cell's own history: jump rate on a cell's first move after Reset vs later moves = first move 50%, after a jump 12%, after an advance 8% (discovery); first move 49%, after a jump 13%, after an advance 8% (held-out).
[C18b] Jumps are much more common on the high branch (5-9) than the low branch (0-4) (C30 shows this is why Area 1 ends mostly 4s): high branch, first move 63%, high branch, later moves 19%, low branch, first move 36%, low branch, later moves 5% (discovery); high branch, first move 62%, high branch, later moves 19%, low branch, first move 37%, low branch, later moves 5% (held-out).
[C19] The longer since a cell last moved, the more likely its next move is a jump (time since last move -> jump rate): 0-24 steps 2%, 50-74 steps 7%, 100-124 steps 14%, 200-224 steps 19%, 300+ steps 21% (discovery); 0-24 steps 2%, 50-74 steps 7%, 100-124 steps 12%, 200-224 steps 19%, 300+ steps 21% (held-out).
[C20] Neighbours are associated with jumping but never decide it: jump rate by number of side-neighbours on the other branch = 0: 6%, 1: 12%, 2: 19%, 3: 25%, 4: 32%; across all 8 directions and all neighbour values the jump rate stays between 10% and 24% (a deciding neighbour would push it to 0% or 100%).
